- [ ] **[both]** **"Stats" tab (fun/superlatives).** My read: worth doing, but
      split stats by what data we actually have:
      - *Points-based (available for every round/player):* most points in a
        single round; fewest points in a round; biggest round-to-round swing;
        most consistent (smallest spread across rounds); most extras points;
        most survival holes; biggest single-round team haul; closest team race
        / largest lead; biggest mover (rank change between rounds).
      - *Hole-level (only rounds with individual hole scores — R1 & R3
        best-ball):* most individual points on one hole; best single net hole;
        count of net birdies/eagles; most pars in a row (survival already
        tracks this).
      - *Caveat you flagged is real:* we don't keep full individual stroke
        play every round (scrambles/best-ball only), so hole-level superlatives
        are limited to the individual-scoring rounds. The points-based ones
        work everywhere and are probably the most reliably "fun." Suggest
        starting with 4–6 points-based cards + the R1/R3 hole superlatives.
- [ ] **[both]** **Retro 80s/90s arcade skin (pixel font + graphics).** My
      read: easy and low-risk to *prototype* — it's mostly injected CSS (load a
      pixel font like "Press Start 2P", chunky borders, arcade palette, maybe a
      subtle CRT/scanline overlay), fully reversible. The catch is
      *readability*: pixel fonts are wide and rough on dense tables
      (leaderboards, scorecards). Recommended approach: apply the pixel font to
      titles/headers/score cards and keep table body text legible, and put it
      behind a toggle so we can A/B it and bail if it looks awful. Streamlit's
      built-in dataframes are the hardest to fully restyle, so expect those to
      stay relatively plain. Quick to try; say the word and I'll spin up a
      toggleable prototype.
